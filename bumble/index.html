01Research

The original goal

The class brief aimed to increase premium conversion through proximity alerts, notifying users when a match was within 0.5 km.

My approach

I explored a different path toward that goal: creating more value within existing matches. DeepSync uses three short, gamified rounds to help two people share their priorities and find something to talk about.

A match is only the start. Keeping the conversation going is the hard part.

What public research and company statements suggest about chats after a match. I didn’t run interviews for this project, and none of these sources tests DeepSync.

Patterns I took from these sources

  1. 01

    Every new match restarts the conversation from zero.

  2. 02

    People want deeper talk but wait for the other person to go first.

  3. 03

    Pressure and time limits make chatting feel like a task.

Sources & limits

Later in 2026, after this project, Bumble let anyone message first and extended the reply window from 24 to 72 hours. It cited a 2024 internal survey of 2,267 women users: two-thirds preferred men to message first because it felt less pressured. Fast Company ↗

Quotes come from company staff, a coach, and published reporting, not from users I interviewed. None of these sources tests DeepSync.

Design direction

Low-stakes activities make room for conversations about what matters.

The idea was to make sharing feel approachable, then turn those responses into conversation. If that experience gives people a worthwhile reason to return to the same chat, it could support retention.

02Prototype

Start from chat, share, then compare.

DeepSync runs as three rounds, each centered on one topic. Light game elements shape the shared part, and AI turns it into conversation.

Between steps 3 and 4, the card shows you’ve finished while the other person hasn’t yet.

Three design decisions

  1. 1

    Keep each step smallA round is a few taps: rank a short list and submit. Taking part stays light enough to do between other things.

  2. 2

    Make shared progress visibleThe progress hexagons show how far you’ve come, and the waiting card shows where the other person is.

  3. 3

    Create the insight togetherThe comparison appears only after both people share, so what comes out of it belongs to both of them.

06Reflection & next steps

Designing for connection, not completion.

Three rounds create a shared rhythm, but can also introduce pressure. The next challenge is balancing meaningful participation with freedom to engage at your own pace.

Next: Shared priorities in discovery

Could shared priorities help people find meaningful matches before they start chatting? An opt-in concept that highlights common values without compatibility scores.

Concept exploration · Not tested
Closing

A match only matters if the conversation goes somewhere.

DeepSync explores how shared activities can turn early conversations into more meaningful exchanges. Instead of giving people another compatibility score, it creates moments of mutual discovery and brings those insights back into chat.

The bigger lesson for B2C design is that engagement isn’t the same as value. Completing three rounds means little if people don’t continue talking. The next step is to test whether these interactions lead to more reciprocal conversations, without adding pressure to participate.
